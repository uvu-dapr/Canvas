Presentations and Sessions: Common Mistakes and How to Hear Them

3 Credit(s)  |  Digital Media Audio Production  |  Utah Valley University

These are the five that cost marks every semester. None of them is about mixing ability. All five are avoidable in the twenty minutes before you present.

Each mistake, the sound that gives it away, and what to do instead
The mistakeWhat you hearWhat to do instead
Running out of time before the second mix playsThe talk ends with an apology and a rushed thirty seconds of audio.Rehearse with a timer once. If you cannot get to the second mix by minute five, cut the setup, not the listening.
Playing the two mixes at different levelsThe louder one sounds better to everyone in the room, including you.Match loudness before you present. The point is the difference in decisions, not in gain.
Talking over the audioNobody can evaluate the mix and follow your sentence at the same time.Set up the listen, stay quiet through it, then explain.
Describing every change you madeTen minutes of small moves with no thread. The audience cannot tell which mattered.Pick three decisions. Say why each one was made and what it cost.
Files that will not open on the room machineA session that needs a plug-in the lab does not have, or a cloud link that will not load.Bring bounced WAVs on a drive. Treat the session as a bonus, not the plan.

The check that catches most of it

The day before, put both bounces on the drive you are bringing, walk to a different machine, and play them from that drive. If both open and both sound the way you expect, you are ready. That one walk catches missing files, wrong sample rates and silent tails.
