Orientation: Handing Work In

3 Credit(s)  |  Digital Media Audio Production  |  Utah Valley University

Every graded item in this course is handed in through Canvas, and each one wants a specific format. Getting the format wrong is the most common reason a mix that sounded fine loses marks, so read this once now and check back against it whenever something is due.

What each kind of work looks like when it is handed in

What to submit for each kind of work, and in what format
What you madeWhat to hand inWhere it goes
A mix you bouncedStereo interleaved WAV, 48 kHz, 24 bit, bounced from the session. Never a screen recording and never an MP3.Canvas assignment, file upload
A Pro Tools sessionZip the whole session folder, not just the .ptx. The audio files live beside it and the session is useless without them.Canvas assignment, file upload

Naming

Name every file so that it still makes sense in a folder of thirty others: your last name, then the assignment, and nothing else. No dates, no version numbers. A file called Final.wav tells me nothing and there will be twenty of them.

Before you upload

Play the bounce back from your desktop before you hand it in. Listen to the first second and the last second. A truncated tail and a silent file look identical in Canvas and are only obvious when you play them, which is what I will do when I grade it.

Late work follows the late policy in the syllabus. Canvas timestamps the upload and not the moment you started, so leave yourself room.
