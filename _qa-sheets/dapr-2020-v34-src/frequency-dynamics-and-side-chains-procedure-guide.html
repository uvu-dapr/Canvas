The Signal Path, Before You Touch a Control

A side-chain is two signal paths, not one. The trigger track does the pushing. A key send carries a copy of it to the side chain input on the compressor, and the compressor, inserted on the ducked track, turns that key signal into gain reduction. You never hear the trigger through the compressor; you hear the ducked track dip each time the trigger hits. Read the diagram in number order before you open a single menu.

The five numbered points in the diagram, in signal order
1Trigger track (the kick or voice that does the pushing)
2Key send off the trigger track
3Side chain (key) input on the compressor
4The compressor, inserted on the ducked track
5The compressor's output: its gain reduction pulls the ducked track down each time the trigger hits

In Pro Compressor, point 3 is the Source menu in the Side-Chain section, shown in the screenshot at the top of this page. Set it to the bus the key send feeds, not to the ducked track's own signal.

Step-by-Step Workflow

  1. Identify the masking relationship and decide which source should trigger control of the other source.
  2. Insert a dynamics processor on the controlled track, enable its key input, and route the trigger source to that key or side-chain bus.
  3. Filter the key signal when needed so only the frequency range causing the conflict drives gain reduction.
  4. Adjust threshold, ratio, attack, and release while listening to the full mix. Confirm that gain reduction occurs only when the trigger is present.
