Presentations and Sessions: Building a Ten Minute Presentation

3 Credit(s)  |  Digital Media Audio Production  |  Utah Valley University

The presentation is worth 100 points and it is ten minutes. That is short. The single most common failure is running out of time before you have played the second mix, which means the audience never hears the thing the whole talk exists to show.

A shape that fits in ten minutes

A ten minute running order that leaves room for both mixes
TimeWhat is happening
0:00 to 1:00Name the song, the problem you set out to solve, and what you want us listening for. No history, no apologies.
1:00 to 3:00Play the week 05 version. Do not talk over it. Let the room hear what you started with.
3:00 to 5:00Play the week 13 version of the same material. Same level, same playback system, no talking.
5:00 to 8:00Walk through the three decisions that made the difference, in the order you made them. Show the session or the plug-in for each one.
8:00 to 10:00What you would do next if you had another week, and questions.

Level match before you present, not during

Bounce both versions, load them into a session side by side, and match their loudness before you walk into the room. An A and B where one is two decibels louder is not a comparison, it is a loudness test, and the louder one always wins. Match them and the actual differences become audible.

What to bring

Both bounces as files on a drive, not streamed and not in a cloud folder you have to log in to reach. A session you can open if someone asks to see a specific move. Nothing else. Slides are optional and usually get in the way of listening.

Talking while audio plays

Do not. The room cannot listen to a mix and to you at the same time, and the mix is what you are being graded on. Say what to listen for, play it, then talk.
