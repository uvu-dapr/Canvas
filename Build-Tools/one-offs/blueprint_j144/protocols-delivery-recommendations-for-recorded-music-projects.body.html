Delivery Recommendations for Recorded Music Projects

What this is. The Recording Academy Producers and Engineers Wing publishes Delivery Recommendations for Recorded Music Projects (2025 edition). It is the industry's agreed way to name, organize, hand over and archive a music project. Labels and clients expect it. This page is the short version: the points you need to know and use. For how to name and hand in work in our classes, see Protocols: Delivery and File Naming Standards, which also links the official PDFs.

Why it exists

  • Recreate or repurpose the work later. Anyone with the files can rebuild the final mix, remix it, or make an immersive version.
  • Keep files organized from the first session to final delivery, with one folder layout everyone recognizes.
  • Preserve the recording long term. Recordings that are lost cannot be replaced.
  • Get credited and paid. Your name in the metadata is your paycheck and your business card.

Masters and backups

  • A Master is the set of original parts of a production, in their original format, ready for the next stage (tracking to mixing, mixing to mastering, mastering to distribution).
  • Three copies, at least two physical locations. One copy on one drive is not a backup.
  • Deliver the masters plus two backups on two different kinds of media.
  • Do not delete useful material: outtakes, talkback, unfinished and unreleased takes stay with the masters (what counts is set by contract, with the artist's approval).
  • Never resample, change level, truncate, dither or noise-shape the primary master when you make a backup.

Formats

  • Broadcast Wave (BWF) files, consolidated. BWF is a WAV that also carries a timecode stamp and metadata.
  • 48 kHz or higher, 24-bit or higher for professional delivery (48, 88.2, 96, 176.4, 192 kHz and up).
  • All documentation and metadata delivered as PDF.
  • Cloud delivery: use a mature, secure service and password-protect uploads. Some services change a file's creation date to the download date.

In your classes: hand in exactly the format each assignment asks for. The professional standard on this page is what a label or client will ask for when you deliver for real.

File naming

  • Legal characters only: A to Z, a to z, 0 to 9, underscore ( _ ) and hyphen ( - ).
  • No spaces, no punctuation, no symbols (no @ $ & # * or parentheses), no accents, no emoji.
  • One period only, right before the file extension. Always show the extension.
  • 128 characters maximum. Keep song titles under 15 characters if you can.
  • Capitalize the first letter of each word: SongTitle.
  • Dates use the ISO order, YYYYMMDD.
  • No aliases, shortcuts or links in file names or folders.

Stereo mix versions to deliver

All versions come from the Approved Mix Master:

  • Approved Mix Master: the final mix approved by artist, producer and engineer.
  • Vocal Up: the same mix with the vocal raised.
  • No Lead Vocal: backing vocals stay, lead vocal removed (once called the "TV Track").
  • Instrumental: no vocals at all.
  • Lead Vocal Only With Effects ("Wet"): the lead vocal and its effects, a cappella.

Naming pattern: ArtistInitials_SongTitle_MixerInitialsMixRevision_MixVersion_sampleratebitdepth.wav

  • A recalled mix is a new delivery: new versions, new version folder.
  • Deliver the mix at the same sample rate and bit depth as the source files, or higher.

Stems

  • Stems are the parts of the approved mix. Summed together they should come as close as possible to the mix.
  • Every stem starts and ends at the same point so they line up when imported into a new session.
  • Number the files so related stems stack together: 01_ArtistInitials_SongTitle_MixerInitialsMixRevision_StemName_sampleratebitdepth.wav
  • Print each instrument separately with its effects.
  • Split drums into kick, snare, toms, overheads, room and effects.
  • Group sections (strings, horns, choir, background vocals) by section and arrangement.
  • Print the lead vocal and its effects as separate stems (for example dry, reverbs, delays).
  • Mute everything except the stem you are printing, so analog-style noise plug-ins do not stack up.
  • Watch stereo-imaging plug-ins: they can cause problems when stems are reused for immersive mixes.

The Key Mix: printing stems through a 2-bus compressor

The full mix pushes the 2-bus compressor harder than any single stem does, so stems printed through it will not add back up to the mix. The fix is to drive the compressor's sidechain with the full mix every time.

  1. Bypass the plug-ins on the master bus from the compressor on.
  2. Print or bounce the full mix. This uncompressed print is the Key Mix.
  3. Turn the master bus plug-ins back on and set the compressor's sidechain input to the Key Mix. Send the Key Mix to the sidechain only, never to the output. (A mono sidechain gets left and right summed.)
  4. Mute everything except one stem and its effects returns, and print. Repeat for each stem.

Some 2-bus decisions cannot be rebuilt from stems. Keep that in mind when you make creative choices on the master bus.

Multitracks

  • Consolidate everything: tuning, edits, time alignment, Elastic Audio, comps and mutes, so nothing has to be redone.
  • Commit virtual instruments to audio.
  • Export consolidated files to a folder so any DAW can open them.
  • All tracks start and end at the same point. Keep the original timestamps.
  • Record blank channels for unused tracks.
  • Naming pattern: 01_ArtistID_SongTitle_CONS_sampleratebitdepth.wav

Immersive mixes (the short version)

  • Formats include Dolby Atmos, Sony 360 Reality Audio, Auro-3D, and surround such as 5.1 and 7.1.
  • Channel names read as floor.sub.height: 7.1.4 is seven floor speakers, one LFE, four overhead.
  • Check on speakers and headphones. Most people hear immersive music on headphones.
  • The LFE is full range, but renderers and some streaming services low-pass it at 120 Hz. Note any LFE filter in your documentation.
  • An immersive mix meant to swap with the stereo mix must match it in intent, pitch and length (Dolby and Apple: within 50 ms).
  • Dolby Atmos: deliver a 48 kHz ADM BWF master. Reference level -18 LUFS integrated, -1.0 dBTP.
  • Sony 360 Reality Audio: reference level -13 LUFS. Do not change Sony's auto-generated folders.

Metadata and credits

  • Start documenting on day one and keep going. Waiting until the end causes errors.
  • Four kinds of metadata: ownership (who gets paid), descriptive (title, credits, instruments), technical (format, size, how to open), recommendation (genre, mood). Engineers handle mostly technical and descriptive.
  • Credits decide royalties, contracts, Recording Academy voting membership and GRAMMY eligibility.
  • Store documentation as PDF in the song's Project Metadata folder.

Metadata checklist: artist, song title, album title, songwriters and arrangers, producers, engineers (recording, mixing, mastering), performers and their roles, samples needing clearance, country, recording dates, studio and location, source format, mix format, label or content owner, publishers and PRO (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC), and identifier codes.

Folder layout

  • Mixes holds Reference Mixes, Master Mixes (every version and recall) and Approved Mixes (only what the client approved), split into Mastered and Unmastered.
  • Project Metadata holds recording documentation, mixing documentation (setup, recall, client notes) and credits.

Backup is not archive

  • A backup is a short-term copy you restore from. An archive is the long-term, single source of truth for a finished project.
  • Keep it in three places and you own it. Two places, you are borrowing it. One place, you are giving it away.
  • Hard drives and SSDs are not archives. Drives fail, and spinning drives left on a shelf can seize up.
  • If a drive is all you have: store it cool and dust-free, power it up once a year, and keep a duplicate somewhere else.
  • Long-term options are cloud services and LTO tape. Copy to new media over time; every format eventually becomes obsolete.
  • Include an MD5 checksum with deliveries. It is a fingerprint that proves the file arrived bit for bit identical.

Check yourself before you deliver

  • File names use only letters, numbers, underscores and hyphens, with one period before the extension.
  • Every name says artist, song, mixer and revision, version, and sample rate and bit depth.
  • Stems and multitracks all start and end at the same point.
  • Mix versions all come from the approved mix.
  • Metadata and credits are filled in and saved as PDF.
  • The project lives in three places, two of them physically separate.
