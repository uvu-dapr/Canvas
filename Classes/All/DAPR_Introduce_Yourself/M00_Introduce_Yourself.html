Prepare Your Photo

Photo requirements
  • Just you in the photo, not a group.
  • Your face is clearly visible.
  • Resized to 800 x 800 pixels (square).
  • Saved as a JPEG (.jpg) file.
Save as JPEG, not PNG
Canvas rejects discussion replies that go over its size limit. An 800 x 800 PNG can be over 1 MB and will fail with the error "The message size has exceeded the maximum text length." The same photo saved as JPEG is around 100 KB and uploads with no problem.
Resize your photo on Mac (Preview)
  1. Open your photo in Preview.
  2. From the menu bar, choose Tools > Adjust Size.
  3. Set units to pixels.
  4. In the Fit into dropdown, choose 800 x 800.
  5. Click OK.
  6. Choose File > Export, set Format to JPEG, and save.

Preview's Adjust Size dialog

Preview's Export dialog with Format set to JPEG

Resize your photo in a browser (Mac, Windows, or phone)
  1. Go to simpleimageresizer.com (opens in a new tab).
  2. Click Select Image and choose your photo.
  3. Under Dimensions, select pixels and enter 800 for both width and height.
  4. Under Format, choose JPG.
  5. Click Resize, then download the resized file.

Add Your Photo to Your Reply

The way you add the photo determines whether it shows up inline in your post or appears as a download link everyone has to click. Follow the steps below so it displays inline.

Do not use the Attach paperclip
The paperclip labeled Attach at the bottom left of the reply box attaches your photo as a download link. Classmates will only see a filename they have to click. Skip this button entirely for question 5.
Use the Upload Image button in the toolbar
In the reply toolbar, click the small image icon (mountain and sun), then choose Upload Image from the dropdown. This embeds your photo directly into your post so it displays inline.
Steps to embed your photo inline
  1. Click Reply under the discussion prompt.
  2. Paste the template and answer questions 1 through 4.
  3. Place your cursor on the "Photo + Answer:" line for question 5.
  4. In the toolbar, click the image icon, then choose Upload Image.
  5. Drag and drop your 800 x 800 JPEG into the dialog, or click to browse.
  6. Click Submit. The photo appears inline in the editor.
  7. Type your fun fact on the same line, then click Reply to post.

Example Reply

Here is what a complete reply looks like. Yours does not have to match this length or style, just the structure.

  1. What drew you to study audio production, and what type of audio work excites you most (music, film, live sound, podcasting, etc.)?

    Answer: I grew up recording my own songs on a phone and slowly realized the recordings mattered as much to me as the songs did. Music production excites me most, especially indie rock and anything with layered vocals, but I want to get good enough at post-production to also work on short films.

  2. What experience (if any) do you already have with recording, mixing, or audio gear?

    Answer: I have about two years of self-taught GarageBand experience and one semester of using Logic in high school. I own a Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 and a pair of AKG K240 headphones. I have never used Pro Tools before this class.

  3. Who is one artist, engineer, or sound designer you really admire, and why?

    Answer: I really admire Shawn Everett. His mixes on the Alabama Shakes and War on Drugs records feel huge without sounding cluttered, and interviews with him make it clear he treats every session as a chance to try something he has never tried before. That mindset is what I want to bring to my own work.

  4. What do you hope to learn or improve in this course, and what are you most concerned or nervous about in this course?

    Answer: I want to build a real foundation in Pro Tools instead of guessing my way through it. I am most nervous about signal flow and routing, since that is where I get lost fastest in Logic and I assume Pro Tools will be even deeper.

  5. Upload a photo of yourself and share one fun fact about your interests or hobbies outside of audio.

    Photo + Answer: Outside of audio I compete in local disc golf tournaments and I have a small collection of vintage film cameras I actually shoot with, mostly a Pentax K1000 and a Yashica Mat-124G.

Submission Check

Before you click Reply
  • Questions 1 through 5 are in your reply.
  • Each answer sits directly under the matching question.
  • Your photo is just you, face clearly visible.
  • Your photo is 800 x 800 pixels and saved as a JPEG.
  • You used Upload Image in the toolbar (not the Attach paperclip), and the photo shows inline in the editor before you post.
