Mouse and Trackpad Settings

You touch your pointing device constantly through a mix session - dragging automation, zooming a waveform, right-clicking a track for the edit menu. A handful of settings in System Settings control exactly how that pointing device behaves, and getting them right removes a surprising amount of daily friction.

Where These Settings Live

Open System Settings. An external mouse is configured under Mouse, and the built-in trackpad is configured under Trackpad - two separate panels, and each panel only appears in the sidebar while that hardware is actually connected.

Settings That Affect a Mix Session

Tracking speed - how far the on-screen pointer travels per inch of physical movement. A slower speed trades raw travel distance for finer control - useful when nudging a single automation breakpoint.
Scroll direction (Natural vs. traditional) - "Natural" scrolling moves on-screen content the same direction you drag your fingers, matching how a touchscreen behaves. Turning it off flips scrolling to the traditional direction older Windows and Mac systems used.
Secondary click - which physical click or corner opens a contextual menu (a right-click). On a mouse this is usually the right side; on a trackpad it can be set to a specific corner or a two-finger click.
Tap to Click (trackpad) - whether a light tap registers as a full click, instead of requiring the trackpad to physically depress.
Force Click and haptic feedback (trackpad) - a firmer press that triggers a secondary action, such as a Quick Look preview or dictionary lookup. Pressure sensitivity and the click feel itself can both be adjusted.
Scroll direction and Pro Tools zoom.
Scroll direction determines which way you scroll to zoom in versus out in the Edit window. A mismatched scroll direction setting is one of the most common "my zoom feels backwards" complaints in a lab - check this setting first before assuming Pro Tools itself is misbehaving.

The Mouse settings pane. Trackpad has its own separate pane with the same tracking and scrolling controls, plus Tap to Click and Force Click.

Tip: If you switch between a Mac trackpad at home and a mouse in the lab, expect tracking speed to feel different between the two - each device has its own separate speed setting, so adjusting one does not change the other.
