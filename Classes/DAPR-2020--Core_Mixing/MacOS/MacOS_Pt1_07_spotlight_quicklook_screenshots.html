Spotlight, Quick Look, and Screenshots

Three of the fastest tools on a Mac all skip a step other operating systems require. Spotlight skips navigating to an app. Quick Look skips opening one. The screenshot shortcuts skip a separate screenshot app entirely.

Spotlight: Search Everything at Once

Command-Space opens Spotlight - a search box that finds apps, files, and even quick calculations by typing a few letters of a name, instead of navigating to them by hand. Type a few letters, watch the top result update live, and press Return to open it. This matters more than it sounds: instead of remembering that Audio MIDI Setup lives inside Applications > Utilities, typing "audio midi" into Spotlight and pressing Return gets there in about a second, every time, regardless of where the app actually lives on disk.

Tip: Spotlight is almost always faster than digging through folders for anything you already know the name of. Save Finder navigation for when you are actually browsing, not searching.

Typing "audio MIDI Setup.app" into Spotlight brings it up as the top result instantly, no matter where the app actually lives on disk.

Searching "Pro Tools.app" instead - Spotlight also surfaces close matches like Pro Tools First, useful when you are not sure of an app's exact name.

Quick Look: Preview Without Opening

Select a file in Finder and press the Space bar. A preview window opens right there showing that file's actual content - an image, a PDF's pages, or a waveform for some audio formats - without launching the full app that normally handles it. Press Space again, or Escape, to close the preview. This is the fastest way to confirm a file is the one you want before committing to opening a heavier app around it.

Quick Look on a real WAV file, complete with playback controls - confirming this is the right vocal take without ever opening a full audio app.

Screenshots: Three Shortcuts to Know

Shift-Command-3 - captures the entire screen and saves it as a file, by default to the Desktop.
Shift-Command-4 - turns the pointer into a crosshair - click and drag to capture just a specific rectangular area of the screen.
Shift-Command-5 - opens a toolbar with every capture option in one place: whole screen, a window, a selected area, plus timed capture and a place to choose where files get saved.

The real toolbar, Options menu open - Save to, a capture Timer, Show Floating Thumbnail, Remember Last Selection, and Show Mouse Pointer all live here.

Screen Recording

The same Shift-Command-5 toolbar also offers Record Entire Screen and Record Selected Portion. A still screenshot cannot show a stutter, a crash sequence, or a plugin misbehaving over time - a short screen recording can. When documenting a technical problem for an instructor or for tech support, a 15-second recording of the actual glitch happening is worth far more than a written description of it after the fact.
