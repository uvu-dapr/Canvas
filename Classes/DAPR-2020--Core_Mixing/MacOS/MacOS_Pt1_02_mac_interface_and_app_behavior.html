Mac Interface and Application Behavior

Every Mac screen is built from the same handful of pieces, and every app follows the same rules for opening, closing, and switching. Once these rules click, navigating any Mac - not just this one - stops requiring guesswork.

The Building Blocks of the Screen

Desktop - the background layer everything else sits on top of. It can hold files and folders, and shows your wallpaper.
Menu Bar - the strip across the top of the screen. Its menus - File, Edit, and the rest - always belong to whichever app is currently active, and change the instant you switch apps. System status icons live on the right side.
Apple menu - the Apple logo at the far left of the Menu Bar. Unlike every other menu, it never changes no matter which app is active. It holds About This Mac, System Settings, and the Sleep, Restart, and Shut Down commands.
Dock - the row of app icons along the bottom or side of the screen. A small dot under an icon means that app is currently running. The Trash sits at the end of the Dock.
Finder - the file-management app that is technically always running, even with no windows open. Its icon is always the leftmost one in the Dock.
Control Center - a Menu Bar icon that opens quick toggles for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, display brightness, and volume without opening System Settings.
Notification Center - opened from the clock in the Menu Bar or a trackpad swipe from the right edge, showing recent alerts and widgets - a separate area from Control Center.
Trash - holds deleted files until it is emptied. Files sitting in the Trash still take up drive space.

Active Application vs. Active Window

Only one app is active, or frontmost, at any moment - its name appears bold in the Menu Bar and its menus are the ones showing. An app can have zero windows, one window, or a dozen windows open at once. Clicking a different window that belongs to the same active app changes which window is receiving your keystrokes, but does not change which app is active. Clicking a window that belongs to a different app switches which app is active entirely.

Closing a Window vs. Quitting an App

Command-W closes only the frontmost window. The app itself keeps running - its Dock icon still shows the running indicator, even with no windows visible. Command-Q quits the entire app, closing every window it owns and stopping the app completely.

This trips up people coming from Windows.
On a Mac, closing every open window of an app usually does not quit that app the way it would on Windows. The app keeps running in the background with zero windows open until you explicitly quit it.

Switching Between Apps and Windows

Command-Tab - hold Command and tap Tab repeatedly to cycle through an overlay of every open app, then release to switch to the highlighted one.
Command-` (backtick) - cycles between multiple open windows that belong to the single app currently active - it does nothing if that app only has one window open.

Hiding Applications

Command-H - hides every window belonging to the active app. The app keeps running with nothing visible on screen.
Command-Option-H - hides every other open app except the one you are currently using, leaving only your active app's windows visible.
Command-Option-H is one of the most useful shortcuts in this entire unit.
In the middle of a mix, a dozen plugin windows, a browser, and a couple of Finder windows can bury your Pro Tools session. Command-Option-H clears every bit of that clutter in one keystroke, instantly, without quitting a single app - everything you hid is still running and reappears exactly as you left it the moment you switch back.

Before: Terminal, an Audio MIDI Setup dialog, and another app all cluttering the screen alongside Safari.

After: one keystroke later, only Safari remains. Everything else is still running - just hidden.
